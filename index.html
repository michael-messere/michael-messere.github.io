I am a fourth-year PhD student in the Department of Astronomy at Columbia University, working with Greg Bryan. My research broadly concerns the circumgalactic medium (CGM) — the diffuse gas that surrounds galaxies and mediates the exchange of mass and energy between the interstellar medium (ISM) and the intergalactic medium (IGM).

My first-year project, with Mary Putman, used IllustrisTNG simulations to study the kinematic structure of an extended Milky Way CGM. In our work, we quantified the co-rotation of CGM gas in ~100 MW analogs — finding that the cool–warm CGM is organized in a large-scale, extended, co-rotating disk-like structure — resulting in a published paper (Messere et al. 2026).

During my second-year project, I worked with Greg Bryan on a project focusing on on zoom-in dwarf galaxy simulations, exploring how supernovae feedback and their high specific energy winds shape the CGM.

I have also worked on a few other project during my first few years in graduate school, some of them involving mentoring other students. These include:

  • The satellite quenching anisotropy in the SAGA and ELVES surveys compared to low mass halos in IllustrisTNG (with Columbia University Undergraduate Nicolas Garcia; Research Note in prep.)
  • The role of cosmological and astrophysical parameters in the CAMELS suite in driving the stellar-to-halo mass relation (SHMR) using an emulator trained on the Latin Hypercube 25 Mpc/h runs (with three high school students and Max Lee)
  • The mass inflow and outflow of Milky Way analogs in IllustrisTNG and how the gas is acquired by the star-forming disk (paper in prep.)
  • The role of the Milky Way stellar radiation field in driving the CGM ion distribution (offshoot of my first year project)
  • The role of the meta-galactic radiation field and stellar radiation field in driving the extdned H-alpha disk in external galaxies (unpublished)

Research Interests

  • Circumgalactic medium (CGM) structure, kinematics, and co-rotation
  • Dwarf galaxy simulations with supernova feedback models
  • High specific energy winds and their effects on the CGM
  • Cosmological zoom-in simulations (IllustrisTNG, Enzo/RAMSES)
  • M81 Group stellar populations and tidal interactions
  • High-redshift galactic outflows (z ~ 1.5–2)

Background

Before Columbia, I completed my B.S. in Astronomy/Astrophysics and Physics (with a minor in Space Engineering) at the University of Michigan, Ann Arbor. During my undergraduate years I spent four years studying the M81 Group with Prof. Eric Bell using Subaru Telescope Hyper Suprime-Cam data, and completed an REU at the University of Copenhagen with Dr. Steven Gillman analyzing starburst-driven outflows using KMOS integral field spectroscopy.

Dwarf galaxies are the most numerous galaxies in the Universe and serve as powerful laboratories for studying the physics of galaxy formation at small scales. Because of their shallow gravitational potential wells, they are highly sensitive to feedback processes — particularly supernova-driven winds — which can dramatically alter their star formation histories and redistribute gas into the CGM and beyond.

My current work with Prof. Greg Bryan uses high-resolution cosmological zoom-in simulations to study how different supernova feedback prescriptions affect dwarf galaxy evolution and the properties of their surrounding circumgalactic medium. I am particularly interested in understanding how high specific energy winds — launched by concentrated bursts of star formation — interact with the multi-phase CGM and contribute to galaxy quenching at low masses.

**Key questions:**
- How do different SN feedback models (momentum-driven vs. energy-driven) affect dwarf CGM gas fractions and metallicity profiles?
- What observable signatures in the CGM distinguish between feedback models?
- How does feedback-driven gas cycling regulate dwarf galaxy star formation across cosmic time?

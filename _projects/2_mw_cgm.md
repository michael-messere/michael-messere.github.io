The Milky Way's circumgalactic medium contains a substantial reservoir of baryons that encodes the history of gas accretion, stellar feedback, and recycling over cosmic time. Most CGM absorption-line studies focus on sightlines at high Galactic latitude to avoid the Galactic disk, but this bias leaves a large fraction of the sky — and a correspondingly large volume of the inner halo — unstudied.

In collaboration with [Prof. Mary Putman](https://astro.columbia.edu/content/mary-putman) at Columbia, I have been analyzing UV absorption spectra along low-galactic-latitude quasar sightlines to characterize the warm-hot gas phases of the MW inner CGM. We compare observed ion column densities and velocity structure against IllustrisTNG cosmological simulations to assess whether modern simulations reproduce the observed CGM properties at low latitudes.

**Key questions:**
- What is the covering fraction of warm-hot gas in the MW inner CGM at low Galactic latitudes?
- How do the observations compare to IllustrisTNG predictions for a MW-mass halo?
- What does the low-latitude CGM tell us about the role of disk-halo interaction and feedback in shaping the halo?

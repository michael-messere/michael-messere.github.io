At cosmic noon (z ~ 1.5–2), galaxies were forming stars at rates orders of magnitude higher than today. These extreme star formation rates drive powerful galactic-scale outflows that can enrich the IGM with metals, suppress star formation, and shape the mass function of galaxies. Understanding the properties and driving mechanisms of these outflows is central to galaxy formation theory.

As part of an REU at the [Niels Bohr Institute](https://www.nbi.ku.dk/english/) (University of Copenhagen) with [Dr. Steven Gillman](https://www.nbi.ku.dk/english/staff/?pure=en/persons/steven-gillman), I analyzed integral field spectroscopy from the K-band Multi Object Spectrograph (KMOS) on the VLT to study starburst-driven outflows in massive star-forming galaxies at z ~ 1.5–2. By decomposing the emission line profiles (Hα, [N II]) into kinematic components, I characterized the velocity, spatial extent, and mass outflow rates of ionized gas winds.

**Key questions:**
- What are the typical outflow velocities and mass loading factors for starburst-driven winds at cosmic noon?
- How do outflow properties scale with host galaxy star formation rate and stellar mass?
- Can KMOS IFS data resolve the spatial geometry of galactic winds at z ~ 2?

The M81 Group, at a distance of only ~3.6 Mpc, is one of the nearest interacting galaxy groups and offers a rare opportunity to resolve individual stars across multiple interacting systems — including M81, M82, and NGC 3077. The dramatic tidal interactions within the group have produced spectacular streams and bridges of neutral hydrogen, and have likely triggered the starburst in M82.

During my four years as an undergraduate at the University of Michigan, I worked with [Prof. Eric Bell](https://lsa.umich.edu/astro/people/faculty/ericbell.html) using deep imaging from the Subaru Telescope's Hyper Suprime-Cam (HSC) to study the resolved stellar populations and stellar halos of M81 Group members. This wide-field, high-sensitivity dataset allows us to map the distribution of old and intermediate-age stellar populations to extremely large galactocentric radii, tracing the imprints of tidal stripping and merger history.

**Key questions:**
- What is the stellar mass and spatial extent of the tidal debris field around the M81 triplet?
- How do the stellar halo properties of M81 compare to Milky Way-analog predictions from simulations?
- What do the resolved stellar populations reveal about the star formation histories of dwarf satellites in the group?

Not all supernova-driven outflows are created equal. When supernovae explode in a concentrated cluster, the combined energy can drive a "super-bubble" that breaks out of the disk and launches a high-velocity, high-specific-energy wind into the CGM. These winds carry energy and momentum at rates that far exceed the canonical wind models often used in simulations.

This project focuses on understanding how these high specific energy winds — characterized by their energy per unit mass rather than just their mass outflow rate — interact with the multi-phase CGM. Using idealized and cosmological zoom-in simulations, I explore how wind specific energy affects the thermodynamic state of the halo gas, the efficiency of metal transport, and the ultimate fate of wind material (recycled into the galaxy vs. ejected into the IGM).

**Key questions:**
- How does wind specific energy determine the thermalization efficiency in the CGM?
- What observational diagnostics can distinguish high vs. low specific energy wind models?
- How do high specific energy winds affect the thermal and kinematic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich signals from galaxy halos?
